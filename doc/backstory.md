# Opening narrative (draft)

Shown at the start of a new game, in the manner of the opening story of a
console adventure or strategy game. Two or three short paragraphs. The tone
is plain; the wonder comes from what the characters believe, not from the
prose.

## Draft

In the last century of the old world, two kinds of engineering came of age
together: machines too small to see, and the means to rewrite living things.
Both were built for war. Both were used. The war that followed was fought
everywhere at once, and when it ended no one had won.

The survivors climbed out of their shelters into a world with no power, no
roads, and no one to answer a call for help. Schools closed and were never
reopened. Books went into the fire to keep the cold out. The creatures that
had been made for sport and for battle did not die with the people who made
them. The machines in the blood did not stop either. They had been built to
repair and to change bodies, and they kept doing it, to the children as well
as the parents.

Two generations later there are seven peoples where there was one, and almost
no one who remembers what chemistry was, or why it works. Some still know a
little: how to mix two powders into fire, how to find a stranger's meaning
without knowing their words. The rest call them wizards, or saints, and they
are not entirely wrong to wonder. As the old saying went, any sufficiently
advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic.

## Notes

- The saying is Arthur C. Clarke's third law: "Any sufficiently advanced
  technology is indistinguishable from magic."
- "Seven peoples" matches the seven species: dwarf, elf, gnome, goliath,
  halfling, human, orc.
- Religion is deliberately left out of these three paragraphs. It is central
  to the setting but its design is open; a fourth paragraph can introduce it
  later.
