I want to build a game with a backstory line inspired by David Brin's novel _The Postman_, but with the following departure points
* Where _The Postman_ imagines technology as the determining factor in civilization and rebuilding, and it is certainly important, it misses the key role of religion. 
* In the 1980s as he was writing we did not really envision the full horrors that genetic engineering could produce. _The Postman_, being technocratic in nature, is rather hopeful about it in many ways. I am less so. Imagine that it produced horrors as well as super powers. Animals could be crafted for both sport and war that, as society fell apart, could become threats to continued survival that accelerate the decline. 
* In many ways _The Postman_ explores a happy path, the protagonist discovers pockets of people who did not fully fall, and that becomes the core of rebuilding. But an industrialized culture requires a great deal of interconnected infrastructure. Imagine that even if society held together, it regressed greatly. It is easy to see how people might have to rediscover each milestone of technology along the way even if they know things are possible from legend that is already fading to myth.
* Part of me would like to take this in a science fiction direction, like the Mr. Brin did in _The Postman_, and keep the horrors to genetic engineered monsters.  Part of me, nostalgic for things like 'Castle of the Winds' would like to go in a more fantasy direction, let the backstory disaster introduce some elements similar to what Marvel Comics does with characters like 'The Hulk' and let pseudo-science clearly deviate into the fantastic, perhaps even the magical aka 'The X-Men.'


Using Perl 5.42 features and higher, with the [SDL3](https://metacpan.org/pod/SDL3) bindings, let's create a game around this. 

We should use Mooish::Base for an object system. 

I also recently came across, from recommendations, some LitRPG inspired web novels bring some D&D style concepts to stories. I haven't really played a lot of D&D, but it came to me that this is a good fit for what I want. That the monsters aren't all equally hard, because a juvenile is easier than an adult is different than one growing old (old is more experienced, but less durable and perhaps less strong depending). The player's units advance and change in statistics in a D&D character style. The tech tree might influence a civilization by unlocking things that affect unit availability, equipment, stats in various ways. The [D&D System Release Document](https://www.dndbeyond.com/srd) might form a useful baseline from which to not have to re-invent *everything* from scratch. Not so much for the specific monsters, but for the structured probability based mechanics and relative statistics. It provides for different sizes of monsters, monsters that might react differently to different things, and have player-like attributes.  the [SRD](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/srd/5.2/SRD_CC_v5.2.1.pdf) provides what is essentially the documentation for an example OO representation of character units, monster units, and how they might interact. 

It will need to play on both OSX (primary dev environment) and ChromeOS. 

Something that you can play single player, or have several players share a map and either cooperate or compete depending on play style. 

This is clearly an ambitious idea. but it should be possible to build it in pieces. a map generator. an ability to have the leader explore where to settle, trying to survive while doing so. tech trees built such that we can add to them as we figure out the right criteria for the next milestone, and the right abilities for it to grant. There are lots of objects that don't need the full thing to test, and many of them can add *something* to the whole even without *everything* in place.
